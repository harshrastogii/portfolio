About

I'm a data scientist in Darwin, building machine learning and spatial analysis on the Territory's environmental data.

I work as an Environmental Data Consultant with Landcare NT, which supports 32 volunteer Landcare groups from Darwin to Alice Springs. I map the land each group looks after, build the app for a multi-year vegetation survey, and set up acoustic monitoring with AudioMoth recorders.

My research is on Northern Territory environmental data: mapping invasive gamba grass from Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 satellite imagery, classifying fire regimes from NAFI burnt-area mapping and weather, and identifying Territory birds from their calls. I test models on data they haven't seen, report results that don't support the hypothesis, and go after the reference data needed to measure accuracy properly.

I came to data science from finance: two years as a financial data analyst at WNS in India, reconciling up to a million payment transactions a day in SQL, then a Master of Data Science at Charles Darwin University, with coursework completed in October 2026.

Research now

Nov 2026 – present

GambaWatch NT: satellite mapping of invasive gamba grass

Independent research · Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2 and MODIS in Google Earth Engine

  • Built a 20 m map of where gamba grass and other exotic tall grasses are likely across Darwin–Litchfield, with gradient-boosted trees on seasonal radar, optical and burn-date features, with labels drawn from the NT Government's 312,667 public weed records.
  • Obtained 27,469 recorded absences from the NT Government Weed Management Branch and tested the map against four aerial grid surveys. Within surveyed gamba country it barely separates cells with gamba from cells without, so the earlier scores were measuring where gamba country is, not the grass itself. The project's claims were restated to match, and the next version trains on those absences.

Jul 2026 – present

Fire in the Northern Territory: weather, fuel and satellite detection

Independent research · TerraIQ

  • Tested fire signals against NAFI satellite hotspot and fire-scar archives from 2004 to 2021. A weather-based fire score that looked useful across the whole record fell to near chance within the same month; time since last burn did far better.
  • Rejected a variable that predicted fire in every month because it was tracking cloud, which suppresses both incoming radiation and satellite hotspot detection.

Fifteen more projects, from fire-regime forecasting to bird-call identification, are on the homepage.
