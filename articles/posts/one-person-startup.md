Over the past few months, I have noticed a growing trend: startups being run by a single founder. At first, this sounds unrealistic. Running a business involves product development, customer support, finances, sales, marketing, communication, operations, and countless other responsibilities. Traditionally, these functions would require multiple employees or contractors. Today, however, AI is changing that equation.

Whenever the discussion around AI comes up, one of the first concerns raised is that it is taking jobs away from people. In some cases, that concern is valid. Certain tasks that once required human effort can now be automated. However, there is another side to the story that deserves equal attention. AI has created opportunities for people who previously lacked the financial resources needed to start and grow a business.

Consider a founder with a strong idea but limited capital. They may not be able to afford an accountant, a customer support team, a salesperson, a graphic designer, an SEO specialist, or even a software developer. A few years ago, these limitations could have prevented the business from getting off the ground. Today, AI tools can help bridge many of these gaps. Founders can use AI to draft emails, create marketing content, design websites, write code, analyse data, automate workflows, assist with customer support, and handle many day-to-day business operations.

## Working beyond your expertise

What I find particularly interesting is that AI allows people to work beyond their original area of expertise. A finance professional can build a software product. A customer support specialist can create marketing campaigns. A salesperson can develop a website. While expertise still matters, AI significantly reduces the barriers that once separated different disciplines. It enables individuals to create and execute ideas that would previously have required a larger team.

Another area where AI has transformed the startup landscape is brainstorming and decision-making. Ideas that might once have required discussions among ten or twenty people can now be explored in a structured and immediate way. Founders can challenge assumptions, ask for counterarguments, evaluate risks, test different strategies, and refine ideas continuously. AI provides instant feedback and allows for rapid iteration, making it a valuable thinking partner throughout the decision-making process.

## AI is not perfect

Of course, AI is not perfect. It can make mistakes, provide inaccurate information, miss important context, and sometimes generate responses that sound convincing but are incorrect. Human judgment remains essential. Founders are still responsible for the final decisions they make, the quality of their products, and the trust they build with customers. AI can assist with execution and analysis, but accountability remains a human responsibility.

One important consideration for founders is the cost associated with AI and digital infrastructure. AI subscriptions, cloud services, automation platforms, and software licences all come with ongoing expenses. Founders need to understand these costs and compare them against the cost of hiring additional manpower. However, even companies with employees continue to invest heavily in technology. Computers, software subscriptions, cloud infrastructure, communication tools, and operational systems are already part of modern business operations. AI is increasingly becoming another layer of that technology stack rather than a complete replacement for people.

What makes AI particularly powerful is its ability to support multiple activities simultaneously. A single person can only focus on a limited number of tasks at any given moment. AI agents, however, can assist with research, content creation, coding, customer communication, data analysis, and administrative tasks concurrently. This ability to parallelise work enables founders and small teams to move faster and operate more efficiently than would have been possible in the past.

## The future belongs to capable individuals

For this reason, I do not believe the conversation should focus solely on whether AI is taking jobs or creating jobs. A more important question is whether AI is increasing the capability of individuals. From my perspective, the answer is yes. We are entering a period where a single determined founder, equipped with domain knowledge, creativity, and the right AI tools, can build products and businesses that once required significant capital and large teams.

The barriers to building have never been lower. The barriers to experimenting have never been lower. And the barriers to turning an idea into reality have never been lower.

The future may not belong exclusively to organisations with the largest workforces. It may also belong to individuals who understand how to combine human creativity, judgment, and vision with the capabilities of artificial intelligence. AI is not simply changing how businesses operate; it is changing what a single person is capable of achieving.
